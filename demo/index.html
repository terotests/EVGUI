            How this page works
            
    
              Every demo is a treefactory declaration naming its own
              tags over the same EVGElement, painted by EVG’s WebGL
              painter. The demos keep their trees between frames rather than
              rebuilding from scratch on every change — a transition remembers
              where a value was, and a freshly built element remembers nothing. On a narrow
              screen a demo is laid out at the width there is, through the
              @media rules in its own stylesheet, rather than drawn at
              desktop width and shrunk.
            
    
            
    
              And read it. A canvas hands a screen reader one empty
              graphic no matter what was drawn into it, so the page also publishes what
              the frame means: EVGA11yFromTree walks the same
              elements and emits roles, names, states and rectangles, and
              evg-a11y.js mirrors those into transparent DOM over the canvas
              — which is what a reader actually walks. Nothing is described twice: the
              roles are on the treefactory tags beside the classes. A reader
              activating a node is answered by pressing the app at that node’s
              rectangle, so it takes the same path as the mouse.
            
    
            
    
              Menus, popovers and listboxes float over the page because EVG has overlay
              surfaces: a menu’s content is still its parent’s child in the tree,
              and layout takes it out of the flow, finds the anchor among its own
              siblings and moves it and its subtree there. A submenu is the same thing
              one level down. A surface near an edge flips to the other side of its
              anchor and slides along the edge to stay on the page.
